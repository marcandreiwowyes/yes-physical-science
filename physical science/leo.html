Who Was Galileo?

A scientist who changed our view of the universe.

Galileo Galilei was born in 1564 in Pisa, Italy. He became one of the most important figures of the Scientific Revolution. He improved the telescope and used it to make detailed observations of the night sky.

His observations of the Moon, Jupiter, Venus, and the Sun provided important evidence for a Sun-centered model of the solar system. His work also helped establish observation and experimentation as important parts of modern science.

A Short Timeline

Important moments in Galileo's life.

1564 — Born in Pisa

Galileo was born in Pisa, Italy, and later became interested in mathematics, science, and astronomy.

1609 — Telescope Improvements

Galileo improved the telescope and began using it to observe objects in the night sky.

1610 — Major Astronomical Observations

He published observations that included Jupiter's four largest moons and features on the Moon.

1632 — Dialogue Published

Galileo published a famous work discussing different ideas about the arrangement of the universe.

1642 — Galileo Dies

Galileo died in Arcetri, near Florence, Italy, leaving a major influence on modern science.

Major Discoveries

What Galileo saw through his telescope.

Jupiter's Moons

Galileo observed four moons orbiting Jupiter. These observations showed that celestial objects could orbit something other than Earth.

Phases of Venus

Galileo observed the changing phases of Venus, providing evidence that supported the Sun-centered model.

The Moon

His telescope revealed mountains, valleys, and uneven features on the Moon's surface.

Sunspots

Galileo observed dark spots moving across the Sun and studied their changing positions.
